Enlarging the philosophical conversation

Distinctive perspectives on character education

Aristotle provides an important philosophical foundation for character education, but other philosophical traditions can help us think more broadly about how education might contribute to character formation.

In this section, we consider the work of three thinkers whose ideas have not traditionally been central to contemporary character education: Martha Nussbaum, Charlene Tan and Simone Weil. Each offers a distinctive perspective on the moral and educational significance of human capabilities, perception and attention. Their ideas invite us to consider how character might be cultivated through intellectual inquiry, engagement with knowledge and our encounters with others.

Throughout this micro-credential, we encourage you to draw on philosophical perspectives of a variety of philosophers to examine character education within your own school context and professional practice. Consider how their ideas might help you recognise opportunities for character formation through disciplinary teaching, relationships and everyday professional judgement, including opportunities that may not be immediately recognised as character education.
