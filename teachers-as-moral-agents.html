Teachers as moral agents

Education is fundamentally concerned with what we value, how we live together, and the kinds of people and societies we hope to cultivate

Every educational decision reflects particular beliefs about what is worthwhile and what it means to live well. These moral purposes shape what we teach, how we teach and the experiences we create for young people. Teachers give expression to these purposes through their everyday professional practice. Consider the decisions involved in an ordinary school day:

  • Whose voices to invite, acknowledge and encourage.
  • How to respond to mistakes, disagreement or injustice.
  • Which qualities to recognise, encourage and celebrate.

These decisions communicate values, shape relationships and influence students' developing understandings of fairness, respect and responsibility.

As Biesta (2026) reminds us, education requires professional judgement about what is worthwhile and how we should respond to particular people and circumstances. This calls for practical wisdom: the capacity to discern what is good to do, for whom, and why.

Through disciplinary learning, relationships and everyday interactions, teachers enact the moral purposes of education and create opportunities for character formation (Kristjánsson et al., 2025).

Teaching is moral work because it involves making judgements about what is educationally worthwhile, guided by a vision of the people and communities education seeks to cultivate.
