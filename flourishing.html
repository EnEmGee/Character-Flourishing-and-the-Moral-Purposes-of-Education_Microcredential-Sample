Flourishing and the purposes of education

Education for human flourishing represents a major shift in the why, what and how of education that will help educate the next generation for their future, rather than our past.

— OECD, 2025, p. 1

The concept of flourishing has gained considerable attention in education, particularly in discussions about student wellbeing. Yet what does it actually mean to flourish?

Although generally understood as living well, flourishing remains contested. Some emphasise happiness and life satisfaction, while others focus on meaningful relationships, moral character, personal development and contributing to the lives of others.

This growing interest reflects a broader reconsideration of educational purpose. The OECD's Education for Human Flourishing (2025) advocates moving beyond narrow economic purposes towards developing human capabilities and contributing to flourishing societies. Similarly, UNESCO (2021) emphasises education as a public and common good, directed towards more just and sustainable futures.

Our understanding of flourishing has significant implications for education. Virtues such as resilience, perseverance and ambition are often encouraged in schools, yet their moral value depends partly on the purposes they serve. Resilience can be problematic in some forms (Trask-Kerr, 2026), while perseverance may be directed towards unworthy goals.

This also highlights the distinction between character formation, which occurs continually through our experiences and relationships, and character education, which deliberately attends to the development of character in light of particular understandings of flourishing.

Ultimately, questions about flourishing lead us to questions about the purpose of education. Are we educating young people primarily for academic and economic success, or also to lead meaningful, ethical and fulfilling lives? And what responsibilities does education have towards the flourishing of individuals and communities?

How we understand flourishing shapes what we value in education, the qualities we seek in young people, and ultimately, what we believe education is for.
